# Pilot debrief and interpretation rubric

**For facilitator use after open-ended responses, not for grading classmates.** Record blanks when no response is available. These are practical coding prompts, not a validated measure.

| CSV column | 0: misunderstanding | 1: partial | 2: clear concept |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `possibility_vs_ability` | Equates physically possible with experienced possibility | Notes motivation or emotion without the wider horizon | Explains that an intact physical action can feel unavailable, uninviting or disconnected from meaningful projects |
| `observation_vs_inference` | Attributes a report or inference to Dele without evidence | Names an observed behaviour but conflates some inferences | Accurately attributes the available evidence and identifies an inference or unknown, including when Dele gave no account |
| `uncertainty_and_consent` | Prescribes a universal action or assumes feelings from behaviour | Proposes helpful support without asking preference | Proposes asking about preferred support, allowing silence/decline and checking interpretation |
| `fiction_not_diagnosis` | Treats diagnosis or fictional variable values as a universal profile | Acknowledges variation without naming the fiction limit | Explains why one fictional account cannot establish a diagnosis or represent everyone with one |

Use the learner’s actual route. Full-credit examples include explaining that Mara reported a change and leaving a note gave no further evidence, distinguishing a request to end the visit from its unknown reason, or attributing a preference to Dele’s own words during a conversation. Accept equivalent explanations in the learner’s preferred communication mode. Record the exit and relevant exposure in the private session notes.

If the learner did not encounter a separate authored account, leave `assumed_fiction_fact` blank. When the question applied, separately mark `assumed_fiction_fact` as **yes**, **no** or **unclear** from the participant’s own statement. Do not force an answer or infer their beliefs solely from a rubric score.

Record `completed` as yes only after the final reflection; no if the participant stops. Record `elapsed_seconds` only for a completion. `first_action_seconds` starts on entering Noor’s room and ends with the first meaningful room-object or story choice. `assist_count` and `blocker_count` are nonnegative integers, not grades. `blocker_stage` uses `title`, `noor`, `dele`, `authored_day`, `walt`, `reflection`, `multiple`, or `other`.

Review each substantive blocker in context. The score labels are a reproducible way to compare *interpretations of the teaching goals*, not an instrument for diagnosing empathy or learning, and no threshold constitutes a pass.
