# Narrative continuity

|Scene|Character business and turning point|Revision target|
|---|---|---|
|One Window Is Still Lit|Sami opens Ferro’s before most residents are up.|Let shutters, the bus and the work light establish time. Keep a concise accessible description.|
|An Ordinary Morning|Noor owes a sign to the library and has a standing bakery date. Mara already paid for her knot.|Let signing or leaving a piece unfinished matter at delivery. Preserve the opportunity to leave without breakfast.|
|Behind the Door|Noor decides to knock, leave paper or walk away. Dele decides about entry.|Store what Noor did, what Dele said and what anyone later heard separately. A closed door is a complete scene.|
|A Different Saturday|Leena runs a shift, knows a patron’s crossword habits and has an unanswered invitation from Nia.|Keep April and October available only on request. Her afternoon can be about a shelf, a sign or a weekend away.|
|The Same Door|Dele knows the failing edge of his own lettering. He can handle his brushes and decide about Mara’s message.|Keep that expertise present. His evening follows every landing outcome. Noor cannot recount it.|
|The Words Between Us|Walt built the library bench. He wants to finish a chair for Rosie. June knows the household rhythm and sometimes speaks too early.|Let Walt set down the card or redirect Mara to the rough armrest. Retain the birthday as his occasion regardless of speech.|
|Outside Again|Noor has a painting with one window left. Dele may get a knot; Leena may board the bus.|Tuesday’s visit precedes this Saturday. Rosie’s Sunday party still lies ahead. Let people pass without giving Noor a report.|

Continuity ledger for editing: a witnessed act, a character's account and a private recollection have distinct audiences. Check carried bags, cold tea, the letter, the sign, Nia’s invitation and the painting on every relevant route. Check unset flags after skipping. Never fill an unknown with a successful visit.
