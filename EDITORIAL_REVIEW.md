**Status:** Textual source audit only. No compensated lived-experience review, clinical validation, or classroom study has taken place. The fictional Dele material remains open to correction. The 8.4.1 revision does **not** alter action rules, dice, saving, room navigation, or encounter choices.

## The question behind this pass

The learner should recognize a distinction between an action that is physically available, the action appearing inviting or meaningful, and what another person communicates about that action. A fictional scene may illustrate an existential feeling, but a clinician cannot infer it from an observed pause or an unanswered message.

## Evidence and limits

1. **Fusar-Poli et al. (2023)**, *The lived experience of depression: a bottom-up review co-written by experts by experience and academics*, *World Psychiatry*. https://doi.org/10.1002/wps.21111 — A synthesis co-written with experts by experience covers changes in agency, interpersonal connection, time, and recovery, and explicitly describes substantial variation. This supports **plausibility**, not any frequency claim, causal law, or validation of Dele.
2. **Ratcliffe (2018)**, *The interpersonal structure of depression*, *Psychoanalytic Psychotherapy*. https://doi.org/10.1080/02668734.2018.1455729 — Conceptual account of changed senses of possibility and interpersonal involvement. It should not be rewritten as "loss of social connection always closes the future."
3. **Ratcliffe (2015)**, *Experiences of Depression: A Study in Phenomenology*, Oxford University Press. https://global.oup.com/academic/product/experiences-of-depression-9780199608973 — Explores variation in bodily agency, time, hope, and social experience. This is theoretical and interpretive grounding, not a script for a simulated diagnosis.
4. **Phenomenological model study (2023)**, *The phenomenological model of depression: from methodological challenges to clinical advancements*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10658893/ — Participant accounts include conflicting or shifting experiences of capability and connection, which cautions against categorical depictions.

**Limits:** This document reports an editorial desk review, not research with participants. The game does not assess depression, suggest therapy efficacy, or make a player's clicks into measures of empathy. References do not establish that its fictional voice matches any particular lived experience.
